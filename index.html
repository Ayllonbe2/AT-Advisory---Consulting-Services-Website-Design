The method

Every product gets one of three verdicts

Each product in your portfolio is scored on the same KPIs, compared against the others and assigned a clear recommendation, with the numbers behind it and the next step to execute it.

Verdict 01

Keep & invest

The product creates value and the trend is healthy. It deserves budget, a roadmap and targets to keep growing.

  • Growing adoption and active usage
  • Positive, measurable ROI
  • Core to the business strategy
Verdict 02

Reduce costs

The product is needed, but it costs more than the value it returns. I identify where to cut: licences, infrastructure, vendors, scope or team.

  • High cost per active user
  • Oversized licences or cloud spend
  • Overlap with other tools
Verdict 03

Retire

The product no longer justifies its existence. I design an orderly shutdown: user migration, contracts, data and communication, so the savings are real.

  • Declining or residual adoption
  • Negative ROI with no path to recovery
  • Better alternative already available
KPIs I analyse

Adoption and active users · retention · cost per user and per transaction · total cost of ownership (TCO) · ROI and payback · operational and technical-debt burden · vendor dependency · strategic fit.

What I do

An outside view on what your portfolio is really worth

01

Product portfolio evaluation

A structured review of every product and platform you fund. Each one ends with a verdict — keep, reduce costs or retire — and a prioritised plan you can take to the leadership committee.

02

KPI analysis & product scorecard

I define the KPIs that truly measure each product, collect and clean the data you already have, and build a scorecard that lets you compare products on the same terms, quarter after quarter.

03

Cost reduction & vendor review

Total cost of ownership analysis, licence and cloud optimisation, tool consolidation and vendor renegotiation or replacement — with estimated savings before any decision is taken.

04

Product retirement plans

Closing a product well is as important as launching it. I plan the phase-out: user migration, contract termination, data preservation, the team's next assignment and communication to stakeholders.

How I decide

Data first, attachment last

Every product has advocates. The recommendation rests on adoption, cost and return — not on who launched it or how much has already been spent on it.

Be willing to kill your own product

I recommended closing a platform I owned once the ROI and adoption data no longer supported it, and transferred another to a better-fit owner — both defended with numbers in front of senior leadership.

Translate between worlds

I read the architecture with engineers and present the same product as a business case to leadership. A verdict only works if both sides understand and accept it.
